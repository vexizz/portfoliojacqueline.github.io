Tapas' lighter layouts

Web DesignCodingProduct DesignEnergy Efficiency

Tapas is a platform for reading webcomics and novels. I redesigned key layouts to be cleaner and lighter, so stories stay easy to browse and read on older devices while using less energy.

Role
Web Designer & Developer
Team
CEO ∙ 2 Product Managers ∙ 10 Translators ∙ 2 Front-end Devs
Timeline
15 Weeks
Scope
Translating ∙ UX/UI ∙ User Testing ∙ Coding ∙ Research
01

Overview of the problem

Image-heavy pages, old code, busy layouts and constant motion made browsing slow on older phones and tablets. They also used more data and battery than they needed to, which pushed away readers on older or lower-end devices.

02

Context

Tapas readers come back often and read for a long time, on all kinds of devices. The redesign focused on readers who:

  • use older or lower-powered phones and tablets
  • read on limited mobile data
  • binge long series in one sitting, often at night
  • need to find the next episode quickly and easily
03

Goals

To make Tapas lighter, cleaner and easier to navigate, without losing the personality that makes it fun to browse.

  • Simplify layouts: Remove visual clutter so stories and covers take centre stage.
  • Lighten every page: Fewer heavy images, scripts, animations on first load through newer updated code.
  • Save energy: Introducing a dark-mode with energy-friendly colours and less constant motion.
  • Keep discovery easy: Make the next episode and new series simple to find.
I redesigned the core coding language, introducing a collapsible navigation system and a refined visual language. By balancing high-density data with a minimalist UI, the new model allowed devices to process sites with significantly less requirements.
04

Process & key insights

I audited existing pages to find what slowed them down, applied sustainable web design principles to new layouts, then coded the key pages myself so I could check how they actually performed on older devices, not just how they looked in Figma.

  • Clean is fast: Removing decorative clutter improved both speed and readability.
  • Images are the heaviest part: Smarter image sizes and lazy loading made the biggest difference.
  • Coding the design reveals the truth: Building it showed problems that static mock-ups hid.
05

Results of what changed

The redesigned layouts gave Tapas:

  • Cleaner pages where stories and covers stand out
  • Lighter pages that load smoothly on older devices
  • A calmer, more energy-friendly reading experience
    • +27%Monthly viewers
    21×Energy Efficiency
06

Reflection of what I learned

This project showed me that sustainable design and good UX usually point in the same direction: less clutter, lighter pages and clearer paths help both the planet and the reader. Coding my own designs also gave me a much better feel for the cost of every visual decision.
