Medication tracker

AccessibilitySelf-initiated6 weeksConcept

A self-initiated project exploring how a medication app could work beautifully for older adults with low vision, designed with them rather than for them.

Role
Solo designer
Participants
8 older adults, 2 carers
Timeline
6 weeks · 2023
Tools
Figma, paper, VoiceOver
01

Overview Why this project

My grandmother manages seven daily medications with a paper chart and a magnifying glass. Every app she tried had tiny text, hidden gestures and alarms she couldn't hear. I wanted to see what a genuinely inclusive version could look like.

02

Co-design Designing with, not for

Rather than starting from personas, I ran paper co-design sessions in participants' homes, where they actually take their medication.

  • Ran 8 in-home sessions with participants aged 68–91
  • Tested type sizes and contrast with printed cards in real lighting
  • Interviewed 2 carers about how they help with medication
03

Design Big, bold and forgiving

The final concept centres on one screen: what to take now. Huge tap targets, photos of the actual pills, voice reminders and an undo for every action.

  • Designed a single "now" screen with 72px+ tap targets
  • Used photos of real pills instead of generic icons
  • Met WCAG AAA contrast throughout and tested with VoiceOver
  • Added a carer view so family can check in remotely
Finally, something that doesn't make me feel old. Participant, 84
04

Results Testing the concept

I tested a clickable prototype with the same participants a few weeks later.

8/8Completed core tasks unaided
6.4/7Average ease-of-use score
0Requests for a magnifier
05

Reflection What I learned

Designing for the edges made the product better for everyone. Many of these patterns, like real photos and generous undo, have since found their way into my client work.
